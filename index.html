Why a solo practice

Small on purpose. Accountable by default.

Most software projects don't fail on technology — they fail in the gaps between people. A single, senior point of ownership removes those gaps. Decisions are faster, context is never lost, and the quality bar doesn't depend on who got staffed this week.

Process

Clear steps. No surprises.

A lightweight process built around transparency: you always know what's being built, why, and what comes next.

  1. Discover

    A focused conversation about your goals, constraints and what success looks like. Honest advice — including when not to build.

  2. Plan

    A written proposal with scope, architecture, milestones and cost. Clear enough to make a decision on.

  3. Build

    Iterative delivery with regular demos and working software early. You see progress, not status reports.

    Clean code, documentation and deployment in your own accounts. Ongoing support available if you want it.

FAQ

Good questions.

Working with an independent engineer is different from hiring an agency. Here's what clients usually ask first.

Why work with a solo engineer instead of an agency?

You get senior expertise on every part of your project, direct communication, and lower overhead. Agencies often sell senior talent and staff junior engineers; with Technull, the expert you talk to is the one doing the work.

What if you become unavailable?

Continuity is built into how I work. Everything lives in your own repositories and cloud accounts, code is written to be read by others, and every project ships with documentation for setup, deployment and architecture — so any competent engineer can pick it up.

Can a single person handle my project?

For many projects, yes — and often faster than a larger team, because there's no coordination overhead. If your project genuinely needs more people, I'll tell you upfront during discovery rather than overpromise.

Who owns the code?

You do. All source code, infrastructure and intellectual property created for your project belong to you, and are delivered in accounts you control.

How do we get started?

Send a short email describing what you're working on. I'll reply to set up an introductory call, and if it's a good fit you'll receive a written proposal with scope, timeline and pricing.
